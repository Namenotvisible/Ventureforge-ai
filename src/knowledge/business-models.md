# Business Models

## What Is a Business Model?
A business model describes how an organisation creates, delivers, and captures value. It answers three questions: What do we offer? Who do we serve? How do we make money?

## Business Model Canvas (BMC) — 9 Elements
1. **Value Propositions**: The bundle of products/services that create value for a specific customer segment.
2. **Customer Segments**: The groups of people or organisations the company serves (mass market, niche, segmented, diversified, multi-sided platform).
3. **Channels**: How the company reaches and delivers to its customer segments (web, mobile app, retail, direct sales, partnerships).
4. **Customer Relationships**: Type of relationship established with each segment (self-service, automated service, communities, co-creation, personal assistance).
5. **Revenue Streams**: How the company generates cash from each customer segment.
6. **Key Resources**: The most important assets required (physical, intellectual, human, financial).
7. **Key Activities**: The most important things the company must do to operate its business model.
8. **Key Partnerships**: The network of suppliers and partners that make the business model work.
9. **Cost Structure**: All costs incurred to operate the business model.

## Revenue Model Types
- **Subscription / SaaS**: Recurring monthly or annual fee. Predictable MRR. Common in B2B software. Example: ₹499/month per user.
- **Transaction / Commission**: Fee per completed transaction. Common in marketplaces. Example: 5–15% take rate.
- **Freemium**: Basic tier free, premium features paid. Works when network effects or usage lock-in exist.
- **Advertising**: Revenue from displaying ads to users. Requires large user base. Common in media and consumer apps.
- **Licensing**: Fee for using intellectual property or technology. Common in pharma, deep tech.
- **Pay-per-use / Consumption**: Charged based on usage. Common in cloud services and APIs.
- **Direct Sales (B2B)**: High-touch sales to enterprises. High CAC but high LTV.
- **Hardware + Services**: One-time hardware sale plus recurring service revenue.

## Pricing Strategies
- **Cost-plus pricing**: Price = cost + markup. Simple but ignores willingness to pay.
- **Value-based pricing**: Price based on perceived customer value. Highest margin potential.
- **Competitive pricing**: Priceed relative to competitors. Safe but limits differentiation.
- **Penetration pricing**: Start low to gain market share, raise prices later.
- **Tiered pricing**: Multiple packages (Starter, Growth, Enterprise) to serve different segments.

## Marketplace Business Models
Two-sided marketplaces connect supply (providers) and demand (buyers). Key dynamics:
- Chicken-and-egg problem: need both sides to launch. Solve by seeding one side manually.
- Take rate (commission) typically 5–20% in India.
- GMV (Gross Merchandise Value) is the top metric. Revenue = GMV × take rate.
- Examples: Zomato (food), Dunzo (hyperlocal), Urban Company (services), OLX (classifieds).

## SaaS Business Models in India
- SMB SaaS: target India's 60+ million SMEs. Lower ARPU (₹500–5,000/month) but large TAM.
- Enterprise SaaS: longer sales cycles, higher ACV (₹1–10 lakh/year).
- Vertical SaaS: industry-specific software (EduTech, HealthTech, AgriTech). Less competition, better retention.
- Horizontal SaaS: broad tools (CRM, HR, Accounting). Large market but high competition from established players.

## Platform vs Pipeline
- **Pipeline**: value flows linearly from producer to consumer (traditional business).
- **Platform**: facilitates interactions between external producers and consumers. Network effects make the platform more valuable as it grows.
- Platform examples: PhonePe, Meesho, Swiggy.

## Asset-Light Models
Asset-light businesses own minimal physical assets and instead connect supply with demand. Examples: Airbnb (no hotels), Ola (no cars), Urban Company (no service centres). Lower capital requirements, faster scaling, but dependent on quality of third-party supply.
