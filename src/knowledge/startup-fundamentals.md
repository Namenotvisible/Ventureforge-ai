# Startup Fundamentals

## What Is a Startup?
A startup is a newly established business designed to grow quickly by solving a real problem for a large or underserved market. Unlike small businesses, startups aim for rapid scale through product innovation, network effects, or technology.

## Problem-Solution Fit
Every successful startup begins with a clearly defined problem. The founder must answer: Who has this problem? How often do they face it? How painful is it? How do they solve it today? The startup wins by solving it better, cheaper, or faster than existing options.

## Value Proposition
A value proposition states clearly why a customer should choose you over every alternative. It covers: the job to be done, the pain relieved, and the gain created. A strong value proposition is specific, measurable, and relevant to the target segment.

## Lean Startup Principles
- Build a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) with the smallest feature set that delivers value.
- Measure: track one or two key metrics that validate your core assumption.
- Learn: pivot or persevere based on evidence, not opinions.
- Reduce waste by validating before building.

## Product-Market Fit
Product-market fit (PMF) means your product satisfies a strong market demand. Signs of PMF: organic word-of-mouth, low churn, users frustrated when product is unavailable, Net Promoter Score above 40.

## Startup Stages
- **Pre-idea / Exploration**: Identifying a problem worth solving.
- **Idea Stage**: Hypothesis formed, no product yet.
- **MVP / Validation**: Small working product tested with early users.
- **Pre-seed**: First external capital, often from founders, family, friends, or angels. Team of 2–5.
- **Seed**: Product validated with early traction, raising ₹50 lakh to ₹5 crore.
- **Series A**: Scaling a proven model, raising ₹5–25 crore.

## Founder Traits That Predict Success
Domain expertise, resilience, customer obsession, speed of execution, ability to recruit talent, and comfort with ambiguity.

## Unit Economics
- **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)**: Total marketing and sales spend divided by new customers acquired.
- **Lifetime Value (LTV)**: Revenue a single customer generates over their relationship with you.
- Healthy benchmark: LTV:CAC ratio > 3 for SaaS, > 1.5 for marketplaces.
- **Payback period**: Months to recover CAC from a single customer. Should be under 12–18 months.

## Key Startup Metrics
- Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) and growth rate.
- Daily Active Users (DAU) / Monthly Active Users (MAU).
- Churn rate: % of customers lost per month.
- Burn rate: cash spent per month.
- Runway: months of cash left at current burn.

## Common Startup Mistakes
1. Building without talking to customers.
2. Scaling before achieving product-market fit.
3. Hiring too fast.
4. Ignoring unit economics.
5. Overcomplicating the product in V1.
